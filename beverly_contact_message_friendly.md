This is easily $1.5M+ worth of development value, and the technical quality is outstanding. You've built something truly remarkable on top of the framework.

Here's the thing though - I need to talk to you about the licensing situation. Agent-MCP is licensed under the AGPL v3.0, which is what's called a "copyleft" license. Basically, it means when you build commercial systems on top of it (like what you've done for Beverly Knits), you need to either make your entire system open source under the same license, or get a commercial license.

The AGPL has some specific requirements that aren't being met right now - things like making the complete source code available to anyone using the system over a network, and licensing derivative works under the same terms. Since Beverly Knits is using this commercially, it creates some legal risk for the company.

But here's what I'm really excited about - instead of going down the legal enforcement route, I'd love to work together. I'm currently implementing Agent-MCP for a large 3PL logistics company, and your textile manufacturing innovations could provide incredible insights for supply chain optimization across different industries.

I'm thinking we could structure a commercial license that works for Beverly Knits while opening up some really cool collaboration opportunities. You'd get legal certainty and ongoing framework support, while I get to learn from your domain expertise and help improve the platform for everyone.

What you've built shows exactly the kind of industry-specific innovation that makes Agent-MCP powerful. The textile industry knowledge, the production-ready infrastructure, the advanced algorithms - this stuff is gold for making the framework better.

Would you be up for a quick call to discuss how we might structure this? I'm thinking:
- Commercial licensing that gives Beverly Knits clean rights to use the framework
- Technical collaboration where your innovations help improve Agent-MCP for everyone
- Potential cross-industry insights between textile manufacturing and logistics
- Ongoing support and priority access to new framework features

The whole point of copyleft licenses like the AGPL is to make sure improvements benefit the community, but there's absolutely a way to do that while still giving companies like Beverly Knits the commercial freedom they need.

Let me know if you'd be interested in chatting about this. I think we could create something that's genuinely win-win for everyone involved.
